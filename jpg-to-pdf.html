Preparing Images for a PDF

Before combining images, check that they are the correct photographs or scanned pages and that they are facing the right direction. If page order matters, choose the files in the intended sequence and review the resulting PDF before sharing it.

The page-size setting offers A4, Letter, and Fit to image. A4 and Letter place images on standard paper sizes; Fit to image uses a page sized to the image. Choose the option that best matches whether the PDF will be printed or viewed on screen.

Images with different proportions may have different amounts of whitespace when placed on a standard page. Review the finished document to make sure important content is not cut off or too small.

Prepare a clean image sequence

Before creating a PDF, put the images in the order you want people to read them. Give files clear names or select them in sequence, then check the downloaded PDF from the first page to the last. Rotate sideways photographs before conversion, crop unnecessary borders where appropriate, and make sure receipts or scanned pages are not cut off near the edges.

Use A4 or Letter when the document is intended for ordinary printing. These page sizes can leave whitespace around images whose proportions differ from the paper. Fit to image is useful when the PDF is mainly for on-screen viewing and you want each page to follow the dimensions of its image. Mixed image sizes may therefore produce pages with different dimensions in Fit to image mode.

Image quality and document readability

JPG is already a compressed image format. If the source is a photograph, the resulting PDF is usually suitable for sharing and viewing, but small text in a photographed receipt may remain difficult to read. Start with clear, well-lit images and avoid repeatedly exporting the same JPG at low quality. PNG can be preferable for screenshots or diagrams with crisp text and transparent areas, although transparent pixels may be placed against a solid page background in the PDF.

Before sending the PDF

  • Check that every expected image appears and the page order is correct.
  • Open the PDF on a phone and a desktop-sized screen if both audiences matter.
  • Zoom in on names, dates, totals, and other small details.
  • For printing, check page size and orientation before sending it to a printer.
  • Keep the original images so you can rebuild the document if a page needs correction.
