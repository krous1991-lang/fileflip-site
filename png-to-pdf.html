Use PNG for graphics and scanned pages

PNG is often useful for screenshots, diagrams, logos, forms, and images that contain sharp edges or small labels. Its lossless compression avoids adding JPG-style compression artifacts to the source pixels. However, high-resolution PNG files can be large, so a PDF made from many full-size screenshots may also be large.

Transparent PNG images need special attention. A PDF page has a page background, and transparent areas may appear against a solid background rather than remaining transparent in the way they do in an image editor. Review logos and icons against the downloaded PDF to make sure their edges and background look as intended.

Choose page dimensions for the final use

A4 and Letter are practical choices for documents that will be printed or submitted as standard paperwork. Fit to image is useful when you want the page dimensions to follow each source image, such as a collection of graphics with different proportions. If your images have mixed orientations, review every page: a portrait image and a landscape image may need different page layouts.

Quality checklist for a PNG-to-PDF document

  • Make sure each image is legible at normal viewing size.
  • Check that small text and thin diagram lines have not been cropped.
  • Confirm the sequence of pages before sharing the PDF.
  • Open the finished file in a PDF reader and inspect both the first and last pages.
  • Keep the original PNG files if you may need to change the order or page size later.

Combining PNG images into a PDF does not automatically make text searchable. If the source PNG contains text, it generally remains part of the page image unless a separate OCR process is applied.
